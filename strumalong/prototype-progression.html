Strum Along: how should a returning player move up?

Throwaway prototype, 27 October 2026. The 43 real songs, their chord shapes and their pace. Nothing here ships; it is for deciding the rules.

The question. Jukebox doesn't teach and doesn't ask anyone to tick chords. So what can it do for someone who comes back again and again? It only knows two things: which songs they played to the end (and how often) and which songs they starred. Can it use just those to nudge them, quietly, toward slightly harder songs, without levels, locks or lessons?

Proposed rules (each is something to agree or change):

  1. Your chords are the shapes in the songs you've played to the end. A star alone doesn't count: starring isn't playing.
  2. Your pace is the fastest chord changes per minute among the songs you've finished.
  3. A “Your next step” shelf appears after your first finished song. It holds songs you haven't finished that add one new chord at a pace you've managed, or use only your chords with quicker changes (up to about half again your pace). Each card says why, e.g. “+ G” or “Quicker changes”. It stays within one level of you: no harder than one level above your hardest finished song, no easier than one level below the song you finished last. If no song fits, it falls back to the fewest new chords.
  4. Surprise me picks from songs that use only your chords, plus your next steps. A new player gets the first two levels, as today.
  5. Another like this stays as it is (shared chords, at most one level harder), but each card gains the same “+ chord” tag.
  6. Nothing is locked or hidden. All the other shelves stay the same.
