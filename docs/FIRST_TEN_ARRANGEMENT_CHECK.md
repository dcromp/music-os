## Decisions supported by this review

Beginner “main chords” remains a reasonable entry mode, with major/minor identity
preserved. It needs a separate teaching vocabulary: removing extensions can
remove useful finger anchors, while a suspended grip contains no third and does
not become a faithful major/minor transcription by renaming it. An optional
richer shape exercise can therefore be easier to change cleanly than its
simplified counterpart. This is a practice-design hypothesis, not a measured
learner result.

Sounding labels and played shapes also need distinct setup handling. In a
uniform D-standard guitar tuning, a conventional grip name is two semitones
above its sounding root name; that follows music theory, not a measured pitch
check on the selected upload. Wonderwall's capo-2 conversion uses the opposite
shape/sounding direction. Seven Nation Army's non-uniform Open A setup needs an
actual arrangement choice rather than a generic semitone transform.

Intentional **mute**, skipped **attack**, sustained **hold**, and empty/unclear
model coverage are different actions. Current practice dashes mean keep moving
without striking strings. Love Me Do's deliberate silence should get its own
cue if a recording-compatible exercise is introduced. House's six spoken counts
are eighth-note subdivisions; two dotted-quarter pulses per bar remains the
declared project convention. Wonderwall stays beats-only while local meter
changes remain unverified.
